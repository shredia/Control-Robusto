Observability Analysis and EKF-Based Sensorless Control of
Permanent Magnet Synchronous Machine
Xin Wen1, Y an Zhang1, Y ahong Lin2
1. Logistics Engineering College, Shanghai Maritime University, Shanghai 201306, P . R. China
E-mail: zhangyan@shmtu.edu.cn
2. Department of Measurement Control Technology and Instruments, Y antai University, Y antai 264005, P . R. China
Abstract: The observability analysis is presented and a combining method applying extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF) and high
frequency injection (HFI) method is proposed to realize the estimation of rotor speed and position at standstill and in normal
situation for permanent magnet synchronous machine (PMSM). Considering the observability of start-up situation, a surface-
mounted permanent magnet synchronous machine (SPMSM) model is developed using vector control method, then the high
frequency injection methods are carried out to estimate the rotor speed and position accurately at standstill, while the extended
Kalman ﬁlter algorithm is applied to validate the observability. Simulation results are shown that the proposed method can
estimate the rotor position and speed accurately.
Key Words: observability analysis, permanent magnet synchronous machine (PMSM), sensorless control, high frequency injec-
tion (HFI), extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF)
1 Introduction
Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motor (PMSM) has a
high torque to inertia ratio, high speed, high efﬁciency as
well as high reliability and compact size. These qualities
make PMSM becomes one of the most applicable AC ma-
chines and PMSM is therefore gaining extensive research at-
tention in recent years [1, 2]. Since mechanic position sensor
is usually too expensive, which increases the cost and de-
crease the stability of the system, mechanic sensorless con-
trol is becoming a research focus now.
When it comes to the sensorless techniques, many re-
search works have been done in speed and position esti-
mation of PMSM using state observer. Some of them use
the back electromotive force (EMF) to determine the speed
and position of the rotor [3], others with the adjunction of a
Kalman ﬁlter [4] or sliding mode observer [5]. Whereas the
main problem of the PMSM observer-based sensorless tech-
niques is the deteriorate performance in low and zero speed
operation conditions, the usual solution is to inject a super-
imposed signal to the control values in order to generate in-
formation without interfering with the normal functioning of
motor [6, 7]. Those solutions show good performance at low
speed and standstill but are obviously very dependent on ge-
ometrical or magnetic saliency of the rotor to allow extrac-
tion of the position and speed information through ﬁltering.
Thus they are unusable for surface-mounted PMSM.
In this paper, a new state observer based on extended
Kalman ﬁlter and high frequency injection (HFI) method is
used to observe the rotor position and speed. On the one
hand, the injected high frequency signal makes sure the ob-
servability problem at zero speed. On the other hand, ex-
tended Kalman ﬁlter can solve nonlinear equation directly
by numeric iteration. The proposed method combining EKF
and HFI offers an effective mean to ensure the observability
in unobservable region, thus solving the problems incurred
in sensorless control of PMSM and observability analysis.
The simulation results verify the proposed method can reach

6 Conclusion
Using the presented approach, which applies sensorless
control and observability analysis of permanent magnet syn-
chronous machine based on extended Kalman ﬁlter and high
frequency injection, it is possible to estimate the rotor speed
and rotor position while the initial position and mechanic pa-
rameters are not needed. Furthermore, by injecting HF volt-
age, the estimation of extended Kalman ﬁlter is more accu-
rate when SPMSM starts up. Though the problem is that the
covariance matrixes of noises can only be determined by ex-
periment since the noises and disturbances are not known in
practice. The simulation result veriﬁes the performance and
the accuracy of the represented approach in dynamic state
and zero speed.
